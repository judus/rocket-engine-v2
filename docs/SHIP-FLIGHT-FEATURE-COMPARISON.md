### Proposed slice labels

These labels make the comparison actionable without adopting a final roadmap:

- **A — Flight contract and scale:** mode/input semantics, units and reference frames, dimension anchors, initial profile and decisions scoped to the next slice.
- **B — Visible inertial flight lab:** one ship, references, editable basic profile, thrust/rotation/coast/braking and measured traversal; explicit simulation-owner/presentation boundary.
- **C — Assisted flight and rapid capture:** combat/approach controls, high-speed capture, actual/interior load, explicit supplied-resource budgets and shortage behavior.
- **D — Full power/thermal coupling:** generation/reserve/allocation, heat/cooling, shutdown/recovery and equipment comparison.
- **E — Journey and balance:** combined travel/capture/approach, large-distance precision, predictions and Julien's playtest.
- **Later:** explicitly separate scope; maintain ordinary integration boundaries without implementing the deferred system.

C must prove the dangerous rapid-capture mechanic with bounded resource/protection inputs. It must not postpone protection until after accepting assisted-flight feel. D adds the full dynamic resource/thermal model, not the first consideration of resource limits.

## Findings that change what should be carried forward

1. **The useful legacy inheritance is the experience, not the modifiers.** `DamperComponent.applyArcadeMode()` changes mass, inertia modifier and drag. Its example profile uses mass ×0.4, inertia modifier ×0.08 and drag ×20,000. This can create the desired strong response, but it is not a configured capture phase or a separately protected interior. Retain travel/precision modes and equipment management; replace that mechanism with declared assistance/capture/protection rules.
2. **An apparent speed ceiling is not proof of an explicit limit.** The inspected path has drag and thrust caps, but no assisted-speed threshold. Similarly, named idle/cruise/boost states are not a progressive acceleration curve: their configured thrust values are identical, `EngineComponent` initially holds the states container, and input state/event names are not uniformly wired. The new envelope/ramp must be specified and tested directly.
3. **Old power labels do not establish an energy model.** Engine thrust and torque charge numerical magnitudes per call without a declared joule/watt law. `EnergyManager` budgets `component.energyCost` against available reserve, while actual `Reactor.consume()` calls in the inspected demo come from engine/weapon use. Sustained module loads, allocation policy and instantaneous action costs need one explicit ledger. The current v2 J/W trial is a better accounting foundation, but its proportional-sharing/recharge policy remains a demonstration choice.
4. **Configured cooling is not demonstrated cooling behavior.** The cooling component stores a dissipation factor. The inspected demo has no call connecting that factor to `HeatManager.setDissipationFactor()`. HeatManager uses its own global factor and producer-temperature rules. A heat-sink input is routed to `dropHeatSink()`, which is not implemented by the inspected heat manager. Treat these as gaps, not finished modules to port.
5. **Overheating is not the agreed shutdown/reboot contract.** The old base module accumulates an overheat timer, damages health after a grace period and deactivates on breakage. The energy manager's activation condition does not check broken status. Requested state, delivered service, thermal lockout, broken state and reboot therefore need deliberate separation; copying these callbacks would not deliver the intended recovery behavior.
6. **Changing the pilot must not delete the ship's machinery.** Legacy `takeControl()` creates power/heat/engine/damper systems; `releaseControl()` removes them and recreates cargo/shields. The new game should retain the ship and its physical/resource state while changing who supplies commands. This matters for AI, player handover and later walking inside the ship.
7. **Timing and removal are correctness concerns, not heritage features.** `Entity2D.addComponent()` treats its fourth argument as render frequency and does not forward scheduler priority. Component iteration consequently depends on registration in this path. The scheduler loses accumulated elapsed time when it dispatches slower callbacks, and removal re-binds functions rather than removing the registered callback identity. Prefer the current Rocket fixed clock/lifetime contract; do not reproduce these defects for compatibility.
8. **The existing 2D capability proof is not a finished 3D entity facade.** Current `Node2D.attach()` and Stage2D demonstrate the desired typing/lifetime/order pattern. `Node3D` currently owns presentation transforms/hierarchy, with no matching attach/fixed capability API. The ship slice must agree a small simulation-owner/presentation adapter boundary; it must not claim 3D ship attachment already exists or automatically force a second transform tree/framework into the design.
9. **One G gauge cannot stand in for exposure.** Old telemetry reads the force accumulator, which the integrator resets, rather than a committed delivered-acceleration sample; update scheduling affects what it represents. There is no separate transmitted-load stream. The new simulation must report actual ship loads and interior exposure independently, with sampling ownership/timing declared.

## Decisions and acceptance by slice

Review refinements from Ivy #816 and Luna #817: the first visible lab needs explicit experimental assumptions, not final balanced handling. Later capture/protection choices must remain visible without blocking the bounded inertial experiment.

### Before B — visible inertial flight lab

- **Units, anchors and bounds:** choose metre/second/kg conventions, a documented initial hull size/mass/inertia profile and representative distances. A fitted visual facade is not its own dimensional specification. These are experiment inputs, not final ship balance.
- **Commands and control ownership:** declare supported axes/reference frames, thrust/braking and rotation semantics, initial input devices, and the sole command owner/takeover behavior. Reuse existing binding/capture machinery.
- **Propulsion hypothesis:** document the initial ramp, its reset conditions and force/mass relationship. Make the basic profile editable; do not require its final curve before the lab exists. No unexplained speed cap in unassisted mode.
- **Simulation-to-presentation acceptance:** agree the smallest connection to Node3D. One simulation owner holds authoritative position/orientation and physical state; presentation reads a committed pose or interpolates committed poses without feeding the result back into physics. Declare who creates, attaches and removes the presentation and simulation resources. Demonstrate movement and cleanup through that connection; do not create two independently writable authoritative poses or require a general 3D capability framework first.
- **Bounded evidence:** one visible ship, references/rulers, explicit profile apply/reset and repeatable thrust/coast/brake/rotation/traversal checks. Declare any resource fixture or omitted coupling so the lab cannot be mistaken for the complete ship game.

### Before accepting C — assistance, rapid capture and protection

- **Capture hypothesis:** choose a documented initial response curve, completion criterion, assisted target/envelope and cancellation behavior. Tune it in the lab. It must support the deliberately risky few-second capture requirement, rather than silently imposing normal reverse-thruster or comfort limits.
- **Protection and shortages:** declare separate interior-transmission/capacity and allocation/degradation rules, including what capture and protection each deliver under partial or zero supply. Use explicit bounded budgets/fixtures; the v2 demonstration allocator does not decide this policy. These decisions and actual/interior-load evidence are prerequisites for accepting C, not B.

### Before D/E acceptance

D replaces declared resource fixtures with the agreed generation/reserve/allocation, heat/cooling and shutdown/recovery model, including equipment comparisons. E validates combined travel/capture/approach, the chosen system-scale precision bounds and Julien's handling approval. Detailed tuning remains iterative; crew professions, full landing contact and Resonance Run remain later.

These choices should result in ship/game profiles and narrow contracts. They do not justify a universal physics hierarchy, an ECS rewrite, or a second engine scheduler. The slice sequence remains a proposal for Julien's agreement, not implementation authorization.
