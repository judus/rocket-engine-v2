Reading the metrics

FPS, frame intervals and CPU times come from Rocket’s rolling half-second monitor. Peak is the longest frame interval in that window. Frame jitter is the standard deviation of those intervals; lower means more even pacing. A very short minimum alongside a much longer peak indicates callbacks arriving in bursts. CPU timings measure JavaScript work; they do not measure GPU execution or browser compositing. GPU draw average and peak cover the last 32 valid asynchronous timer-query samples of the clear and triangle draw. Results arrive on later frames; a maximum of four queries can be pending. Invalid (disjoint) samples are discarded. Unsupported browsers show “unsupported” rather than an estimated GPU time. These measurements exclude display latency and compositing; CPU and GPU times overlap and must not be added together.

For comparisons, keep the same canvas size, display scale, camera pose and field of view, and keep this tab in the foreground. FPS is capped by the browser/display refresh rate; CPU times can increase before FPS drops. Recreate demo starts a fresh measurement window.

A directional light illuminates two boxes and a hovering sphere. Their depth is rendered into a separate texture from the light's viewpoint. The floor compares its depth against that map; the ambient fill remains even inside shadows.

Try 64² with PCF off to expose jagged edges. Too little bias can produce self-shadow stripes (shadow acne); too much detaches shadows (peter-panning). PCF averages nine depth comparisons to soften the edge, rather than simulating a large light source. Larger coverage shares the same pixels across more world space; outside the fitted volume is treated as lit.
