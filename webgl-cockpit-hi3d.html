Reading the metrics

FPS, frame intervals and CPU times come from Rocket’s rolling half-second monitor. Peak is the longest frame interval in that window. Frame jitter is the standard deviation of those intervals; lower means more even pacing. A very short minimum alongside a much longer peak indicates callbacks arriving in bursts. CPU timings measure JavaScript work; they do not measure GPU execution or browser compositing. GPU draw average and peak cover the last 32 valid asynchronous timer-query samples of the clear and triangle draw. Results arrive on later frames; a maximum of four queries can be pending. Invalid (disjoint) samples are discarded. Unsupported browsers show “unsupported” rather than an estimated GPU time. These measurements exclude display latency and compositing; CPU and GPU times overlap and must not be added together.

For comparisons, keep the same canvas size, display scale, camera pose and field of view, and keep this tab in the foreground. FPS is capped by the browser/display refresh rate; CPU times can increase before FPS drops. Recreate demo starts a fresh measurement window.

Lamp fitting

Each lamp has independent on/off and X/Y fitting controls. Positive X moves right, positive Y moves up. Use arrow keys for fine 0.001-unit steps. Rectangular buttons keep a dark lens when off; R1/R2 retain a faint 2% standby glow. R1 (left round) is orange for warnings; R2 (right round) is red for critical alerts. Colors can change live; these controls are manual previews, not connected to gameplay conditions.

          Isolate removes ambient, studio fill and the existing

          This reuses existing inverse-square point lights, offset in front of
          the five display meshes. Soft turquoise light and face areas
          approximate backlight output. A broader falloff reduces center
          hotspots. It does not sample the animated pixels or simulate area
          lights. These lights have no shadows or occlusion, so they can leak
          through cabin walls. Display power stops their contribution and blacks
          out the five textures; pausing display updates simply freezes the
          powered readouts.

          pass and enabled post-processing.

        Space defaults use one fixed distant sun, 1% ambient fill and no studio
        reflection or inspection point light. Cockpit screens and lamps provide
        local light. The studio map is optional for inspection. This is distant
        environment lighting: the cockpit and grid do not reflect each other,

        direct-light controls independently.
      

      

        
        
        
        
      

      

        
        
        
        
      

      

      

        Every asteroid and the cockpit cast and receive sun shadows. One shadow
        map covers the entire finite field and the moving cockpit. Higher
        resolution improves detail but costs memory and GPU time (4096: 64 MiB,
        8192: 256 MiB). The volume expands if you fly far beyond the field,
        reducing detail. Sun shadows do not affect local lamp spill or glowing
        lenses. Use the visibility mask to inspect actual shadow coverage.
