Reading the metrics

FPS, frame intervals and CPU times come from Rocket’s rolling half-second monitor. Peak is the longest frame interval in that window. Frame jitter is the standard deviation of those intervals; lower means more even pacing. A very short minimum alongside a much longer peak indicates callbacks arriving in bursts. CPU timings measure JavaScript work; they do not measure GPU execution or browser compositing. GPU draw average and peak cover the last 32 valid asynchronous timer-query samples of the scene draw. Results arrive on later frames; a maximum of four queries can be pending. Invalid (disjoint) samples are discarded. Unsupported browsers show “unsupported” rather than an estimated GPU time. These measurements exclude display latency and compositing; CPU and GPU times overlap and must not be added together.

For comparisons, keep the same canvas size, display scale, camera pose and field of view, and keep this tab in the foreground. FPS is capped by the browser/display refresh rate; CPU times can increase before FPS drops. Recreate demo starts a fresh measurement window.

Starting…

128 triangles per rock, shared among eight shape variants. Static rocks; the camera moves. Ambient fill and one directional light reveal the facets. No textures, shadows, collisions, streaming, wrapping, LOD or GPU instancing. You can fly through rocks and beyond the field. This is a content and navigation milestone, not a stress benchmark.
